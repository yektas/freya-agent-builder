- Earliest consequential failure in the trace: PUB-02 extracted “tomorrow” as `2025-05-15` although supplied `now` was in October 2026; its validator could not use that value, and the dialogue read back a different date. PUB-07 extracted “Monday the twenty-sixth” as `next Monday`, causing a write for October 19. PUB-10 extracted the amount but dropped “Friday,” retaining the initial offer date.
- Evidence supporting the proposed mechanism: the recorded extraction, route, readback, and bank-tool arguments disagree on the intended date. These are directly observable in `platform.jsonl` for the baseline run.
- Regressions or missing cases: PUB-06 is an amount-route failure outside the initial date change. New graphs introduced unrelated regressions. The first build's date parser could not be measured: `relaxInvalidEscapes` discarded Python `\d` and decoded `\b` to backspace inside a raw regex. A general compiler recovery fix and focused test preserve these escapes. The source-driven closing build revealed a verified compiler bug: `bindTool` added defaults to optional fields even when the raw candidate omitted them. The boundary fix now keeps those fields unset and has a focused offline test.
- Uncertainty / suspected noise: the documented-extraction build varied from 6/10 to 7/10 across three repetitions; its 4/10 strict score shows that one 7/10 run was not reliable. The direct baseline has only one measured repetition, so its strict score is unknown. PUB-07 failed in all three documented-extraction repetitions and has a deterministic parser cause; other misses were intermittent.
- Decision: reject the measured date-fidelity, identity-isolation, and documented-extraction builds; withhold the two unmeasured builds from practice. Test a new partial-speech and ordinal-date revision while keeping direct as the baseline.
- Reason: the documented-extraction build's single-run improvement disappeared under the organizer's 3/3 aggregation. The next revision targets the trace-supported calendar parser error and interruption/offer state problems without adding case values or a graph-size limit.
